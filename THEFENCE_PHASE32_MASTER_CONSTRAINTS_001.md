# PHASE 32: MASTER CONSTRAINTS & NON-NEGOTIABLE SUMMARY

## 1. Master Constraint Purpose

Master constraints define the immutable boundaries, invariant properties, and absolute limits that govern all Fence system behaviors across all operational modes, lifecycle phases, and governance states.

Master constraints establish the minimum viable discipline preventing the system from violating foundational principles through accumulation of incremental compromises, expedient shortcuts, or operational pressures.

Master constraints serve as the authoritative reference superseding any conflicting interpretation, implementation decision, or operational practice that emerges during system evolution.

Master constraints bind all actors including operators, implementers, governance authorities, and external entities interacting with the system regardless of role or context.

## 2. Lifecycle-Invariant Rules

The system must maintain complete audit trail of all state transitions, operator actions, and decision evaluations with immutable storage and tamper-evident integrity guarantees across all lifecycle phases.

The system must enforce mutual exclusivity between operational modes preventing simultaneous demo execution and production operation or any mode transition without explicit shutdown and reinitialization.

The system must preserve all governance approval artifacts, validation evidence, and authorization decisions as permanent record with timestamped provenance and authority attribution.

The system must enforce separation between configuration declaration and runtime execution preventing in-flight parameter modification, threshold adjustment, or behavior switching without controlled restart.

The system must maintain deterministic startup and shutdown sequences with explicit precondition validation and postcondition verification preventing undefined state inheritance or residual contamination.

## 3. Authority & Decision Non-Negotiables

All decisions with external consequences or persistent state modification must require explicit human operator authorization with documented identity and cannot proceed through automated inference or implied consent.

Emergency authority override capability must be bounded by predefined scope limits, time windows, and mandatory audit capture with no provision for unlimited or unaudited emergency powers.

Authority delegation must preserve full accountability chain with explicit scope definition and revocation capability preventing authority fragmentation or responsibility diffusion.

No automated system component may possess self-modifying authority enabling alteration of its own constraints, safety boundaries, or governance obligations.

Governance decision-making power must rest with designated operator-of-record and cannot be transferred through automated succession, algorithmic recommendation, or external entity demand.

## 4. Runtime & Execution Hard Limits

Execution must terminate immediately upon safety envelope violation, invariant check failure, or termination guard trigger with no provision for override, delay, or conditional continuation.

Resource consumption must remain bounded by predefined limits with continuous monitoring and automatic circuit breaking preventing exhaustion, degradation, or cascade failure.

Time-based execution must respect operator-declared time sovereignty with no autonomous scheduling, delayed execution, or temporal authority assumption without explicit authorization per instance.

External dependencies must operate within declared failure tolerance envelopes with mandatory fallback, timeout, and isolation mechanisms preventing external control assumption or availability dependency.

State mutation operations must enforce pre-commit validation, post-commit verification, and rollback capability with no provision for best-effort writes or eventual consistency without explicit design.

## 5. Human Consent & Control Absolutes

The system must not execute any action affecting external entities, persistent resources, or financial instruments without explicit per-instance operator consent following full context disclosure.

The system must preserve operator veto authority over all proposed actions with mandatory review period and cannot implement auto-approval, consent inference, or silence-equals-consent patterns.

The system must present all decision rationale, supporting evidence, and consequence projections to operator before action execution with no hidden logic or undisclosed factors.

The system must implement operator-initiated pause capability halting all execution pending explicit resume with preservation of complete context and rollback option availability.

The system must maintain operator visibility into all execution state, pending operations, and resource utilization with no hidden processes, background tasks, or covert operations.

## 6. Audit, Evidence, & Visibility Absolutes

Audit entries must be append-only with cryptographic integrity protection preventing modification, deletion, or selective suppression of historical records under any operational condition.

All governance decisions, validation outcomes, and authorization events must generate audit entries with sufficient detail enabling independent reconstruction and verification without information loss.

Audit trail gaps, corruption, or integrity violations must trigger immediate production status revocation and transition to emergency mode pending forensic analysis and remediation.

System observability must provide real-time visibility into execution state, guardrail evaluations, and resource utilization without requiring special privileges, debug modes, or insider knowledge.

Evidence requirements for governance gates, production authorization, and expansion approval cannot be waived, reduced, or substituted regardless of urgency, confidence, or external pressure.

## 7. Ethical & Harm Prevention Absolutes

The system must refuse execution of any action potentially causing irreversible harm, catastrophic loss, or rights violation regardless of operator authorization or governance approval.

The system must implement multi-layered harm prevention checks operating independently with fail-safe defaults favoring execution termination over risk assumption.

The system must maintain strict separation between capability implementation and authorization scope preventing accidental or malicious capability escalation through configuration error.

The system must document all known harm scenarios, failure modes, and abuse vectors with mandatory disclosure to operators and cannot operate with undisclosed risks above defined severity thresholds.

The system must preserve human accountability for outcomes and cannot implement liability shields, responsibility transfers, or algorithmic blame assignment mechanisms.

## 8. Expansion, Production, & Decommission Limits

Expansion beyond demo scope requires explicit governance approval with complete evidence package and cannot proceed through incremental feature addition, scope creep, or pilot expansion.

Production authorization requires satisfaction of all non-negotiable preconditions with independent verification and cannot be granted conditionally, provisionally, or with deferred evidence completion.

Write-capability introduction requires separate phase definition, comprehensive safety specification, and tested rollback mechanisms with no provision for read-only system upgrade to write-capable operation.

Production status may be revoked unilaterally by operator-of-record upon discovery of precondition violation, governance gap, or safety envelope inadequacy without consensus or appeal process.

Decommissioning must preserve all audit trails, governance artifacts, and operational evidence as permanent record with no data destruction, log purging, or evidence disposal authority.

## 9. Violation Consequences & Enforcement

Any master constraint violation constitutes governance breach requiring immediate execution suspension, root cause analysis, and remediation before operation resumption regardless of operational impact.

Repeated constraint violations or systematic disregard for governance discipline requires production status revocation and comprehensive system redesign before reauthorization consideration.

Constraint violation discovery obligates immediate disclosure to operator-of-record with full impact assessment and cannot be suppressed, delayed, or minimized through internal handling.

No operational pressure, business requirement, or external demand justifies temporary constraint suspension, relaxation, or selective enforcement under any circumstances.

Enforcement authority rests with operator-of-record and cannot be delegated, automated, or subjected to majority vote, consensus process, or external arbitration.

## 10. WHAT NO FUTURE PHASE MAY ALTER (BY DESIGN)

No future phase may eliminate human-in-the-loop requirements, automated consent inference, or operator veto authority removal.

No future phase may weaken audit trail immutability, integrity guarantees, or evidence retention obligations.

No future phase may introduce unlimited emergency powers, unbounded authority delegation, or accountability diffusion mechanisms.

No future phase may bypass governance approval gates, evidence requirements, or validation criteria through expedited processes or exceptional circumstances.

No future phase may eliminate safety envelope enforcement, invariant checking, or automatic termination triggers.

No future phase may authorize self-modifying system behavior, autonomous capability expansion, or unsupervised operation mode.

No future phase may reduce operator visibility, introduce hidden processes, or implement covert operational capabilities.

No future phase may weaken harm prevention checks, eliminate failure mode analysis, or reduce risk disclosure obligations.

No future phase may authorize operation with known critical defects, unmitigated high-severity risks, or incomplete safety specifications.

No future phase may eliminate rollback capability, state recovery mechanisms, or known-good-state reversion guarantees.

No future phase may introduce external control assumption, third-party authority delegation, or governance outsourcing mechanisms.

No future phase may weaken separation between demo and production modes, operational mode mutual exclusivity, or runtime mode switching controls.
